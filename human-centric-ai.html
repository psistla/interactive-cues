Human-Centric AI is an interaction problem

The phrase hides three commitments that routinely conflict: human control, human benefit, and human legibility. Treating it as a values statement produces principle slides no one can act on. Treating it as interaction design produces decisions you can instrument and be wrong about. Every load-bearing claim below carries its source and its evidence status.

Claim status Evidence Inference Practitioner Evidence means a published empirical finding, cited. Inference means reasoned from cited findings but not itself tested. Practitioner means field judgment with no study behind it, offered as a hypothesis to instrument. Full references in section 06.

01 · Premise

Three commitments wear one label, and the evidence says they trade against each other.

Control, benefit, and legibility are usually sold as a package. The largest meta-analysis of human plus AI systems to date found the package does not hold: across 106 experimental studies and 370 effect sizes, human plus AI combinations performed significantly worse on average than the better of human alone or AI alone, with losses concentrated in decision tasks and gains concentrated in content creation.

Commitment one

Control

The human can intervene: disregard, override, or reverse. This is not just a design goal; it is the operative language of Article 14(4)(d) of the EU AI Act for high-risk systems.

EU AI Act Art. 14

Commitment two

Benefit

Outcomes measured on the people affected. The meta-analytic result is conditional and that conditionality is the whole argument: combinations gained when the human outperformed the AI alone, and lost when the AI outperformed the human alone.

EvidenceVaccaro et al. 2024

Commitment three

Legibility

The human can form an accurate belief about how far to trust the system. The canonical framing is appropriate reliance, not maximal trust: reliance should track capability, in context.

Lee & See 2004

Reframe

Stop asking "is there a human in the loop?" That question is answered yes by a checkbox. Ask: what does the human's presence change, and can you demonstrate it? Vaccaro and colleagues give you the shape of the expected answer: the human helps where the human is better, and hurts where the system is better and the human cannot tell which cases are the exceptions.

Inference

Falsifier for this section. Remove the human step, hold throughput constant, and measure outcome quality. If quality is statistically unchanged, the loop was decoration and the rest of this deck solves a problem you do not have.

Honest limit on the meta-analysis. It covers experimental studies published from 2020 onward, mostly laboratory tasks with recruited participants. Whether the average effect transfers to production systems with trained operators and real stakes is not established by it. The conditional finding (gains where humans are stronger, losses where the system is stronger) is the durable part; the average effect size is the part most likely to move.

02 · Control

Approve or reject is the weakest control surface, and it degrades into skimming.

A binary gate lets the human say no. It does not let them say how, why not, or almost. And under real conditions the gate erodes. Interviewing developers who oversee software agents in professional work, researchers at FAccT 2026 found that oversight had settled into efficiency heuristics: plans became proxies for the agent's actual working, test results stood in for code correctness, and skimming became the main information processing mechanism.

Mechanism

Grade control by reversibility, not by risk label

Risk tiers are assigned by committee and drift toward caution, so everything becomes high risk and every gate becomes meaningless. Reversibility is a property of the action: observable, arguable, and it changes when the architecture changes.

Inference

Failure mode

Sustained monitoring degrades, and the record does not show it

The vigilance decrement is one of the oldest replicated findings in human factors, confirmed meta-analytically across dozens of studies; review literature places the major portion of the decline within the first fifteen minutes of watch. The gate keeps producing approval records long after it stopped producing scrutiny.

Practitioner

The regulatory gap worth noticing

Article 14(4)(b) of the EU AI Act requires that overseers be enabled "to remain aware" of automation bias. The regulation correctly names the psychological failure mode, then asks awareness to solve it. Awareness is not a mechanism: the whole literature on automation bias describes a tendency that persists in people who know about it. A survey of 41 policies mandating human oversight of government algorithms concluded that people are largely unable to perform the oversight functions being asked of them, and that the mandates end up legitimizing deployment rather than constraining it.

03 · Handoff

Systems fail at the boundary, and the boundary erodes the skill needed to cross it.

Bainbridge stated the structural problem in 1983: automating the tractable parts leaves the operator only the hard parts, while removing the routine practice that built the competence to handle them. It is one of the most cited papers in human factors, and it was a theoretical argument. In 2025 it acquired a clinical measurement.

Unassisted adenoma detection rate, before and after three months of routine AI-assisted colonoscopy

Before AI exposure (n = 795)
28.4%
After AI exposure (n = 648)
22.4%

Nineteen experienced endoscopists across four centres in Poland, 1,443 non-AI-assisted colonoscopies. A 6 percentage point absolute and roughly 20 percent relative decline in unassisted detection, described by the authors as the first documentation of a potential deskilling effect from clinical AI. Read the design carefully: this is a retrospective observational comparison of two time periods, not a randomized test of deskilling. Secular trends, case mix, and operator behaviour changes are not fully excluded, and the finding drew published correspondence on exactly these points. It is the strongest evidence available and it is not conclusive.

Failure mode one

The handoff cliff

A meta-analysis of 129 automated-driving studies found takeover time is driven heavily by what the human was doing beforehand: engagement in visual or manual secondary tasks lengthens it. The person is not idle when the escalation arrives; they are elsewhere, and the transfer has to pay for that.

EvidenceZhang et al. 2019

Counter-move

Budget practice as a cost, not as oversight

The same meta-analysis found drivers responded about one second faster on their second takeover than their first: recent practice measurably helps. Routing a sampled slice of routine cases to humans at a rate tuned to maintain competence is a training expense, and should be budgeted as one rather than justified as error-catching.

InferenceZhang et al. 2019

What the deskilling evidence does and does not license. One observational study in one specialty does not establish that every AI deployment erodes competence. What it does establish is that the question is answerable empirically and that at least one careful attempt found a decline. If your domain has a measurable unassisted-performance baseline, you can run the same comparison. If it does not, you are asserting that deskilling is not happening rather than knowing it.

04 · Calibration

The target is not trust. It is trust that tracks reliability, and self-report will not tell you whether you have it.

Over-trust and under-trust are the same failure wearing different clothes: a belief about the system that does not match the system. The sharpest available demonstration comes from a randomized controlled trial of experienced open-source developers working on their own repositories, where the direction of the effect and the direction of the perception were opposite.

Expected, perceived, and measured effect of AI tools on task completion time (16 developers, 246 tasks, February to June 2025)

20% faster (believed)

19% slower (measured)

Confidence interval on the measured slowdown ran from +2% to +39%. Read the update before you cite this. METR ran a follow-up from August 2025 with 57 developers and 800+ tasks, and in February 2026 published that the newer data gives an unreliable signal: 30 to 50 percent of developers declined to submit tasks they did not want to do without AI, biasing the estimate downward. Their raw follow-up numbers point toward speedup (-18% for returning developers, -4% for new ones) but both intervals cross zero. The honest current position is that the 2025 slowdown figure is historical and no reliable replacement estimate exists. The finding that survives is the perception gap itself.

Intervention that works

Make people think, not trust

Cognitive forcing functions, design interventions that interrupt the moment of acceptance and require engagement, reduced overreliance on AI in a controlled study where simply adding explanations did not. Explanations alone can leave overreliance intact.

EvidenceBuçinca et al. 2021

Why it rarely ships

The intervention that works tests worst

In the same study, participants rated the cognitive forcing conditions less favourably than the simple explainable-AI conditions, reporting lower trust and higher perceived complexity, with the effect strongest among those most inclined to engage analytically. A satisfaction-optimizing product process will delete this feature.

EvidenceBuçinca et al. 2021

Falsifier

Plot override rate against genuine reliability regimes. If the line is flat, your confidence signal is not being read, whatever the satisfaction survey says about perceived transparency. The developer study is the warning: the people in the best position to know were confidently wrong about the direction of the effect.

Selection rule

Pick patterns by which failure you can actually detect. A control you cannot verify is operating is worse than no control, because it also removes the urgency to build one that works. That is the mechanism Green identifies in oversight mandates: the requirement is satisfied, the harm is not addressed, and accountability moves to whoever signed.

InferenceGreen 2022

06 · Evidence

The strongest argument against this framing, what would settle it, and where I was wrong.

Human-centric design has a serious objection its advocates tend to skip: in a growing set of domains the human is the least reliable component in the chain. The meta-analytic evidence supports the objection directly. Inserting a human where the system is stronger produced losses, not gains.

The objection

Oversight can make outcomes worse and accountability murkier

Where the system outperforms the reviewer and the reviewer cannot identify the exceptions, review adds noise, latency, and cost. Green's survey of 41 oversight policies argues this is the normal case rather than the edge case, and that the mandates function as legitimation: deployment proceeds, harm is unaddressed, responsibility shifts to the overseer.

EvidenceGreen 2022Vaccaro et al. 2024

The response

Place humans by measured marginal contribution

The defence is not humans everywhere. It is humans where their marginal contribution is measurable and positive, which the meta-analysis says is where humans already outperform the system on the task. Everywhere else, spend the same effort on reversibility and on the handoff, and say plainly that the human is not there for accuracy.

Corrections made in this edition. Four claims from the earlier unsourced version did not survive checking, and are named here rather than quietly removed.

1. "Approval rates converge to 100 percent under volume" had no source and is removed. The defensible version is narrower: developers overseeing agents report skimming as their main processing mechanism, and substituting plans and test results for direct review.

2. "The second and third interruptions in an hour cost far more than the first" is not supported by the interruption literature I checked and is removed. The finding that replaces it is more useful anyway: interrupted work finished no slower and no worse, at significantly higher stress and effort, so completion-time metrics systematically underprice interruption.

3. "Undo beats confirm" was presented as established. It is an inference and is now labelled as one. Two-phase staging appears in the 2026 agentic-architecture literature as a proposed tactic, not a measured result.

4. The vigilance claim was stated too loosely. The decrement is robust and meta-analytically confirmed, but its mechanism is contested: whether it reflects genuine loss of perceptual sensitivity or a shift in response criterion has been challenged in the modern literature, and the distinction matters for whether better displays can fix it.

Epistemic status of the whole. The human factors foundations (vigilance decrement, automation bias, the ironies of automation) are established across four decades and multiple domains. The contemporary empirical anchors (the meta-analysis, the deskilling study, the developer trial, the agent-oversight interviews) are each single studies or single syntheses with named limitations, and three of the four are under active revision or correspondence. The design patterns are practitioner synthesis and are labelled as such throughout.

Weakest assumption, stated plainly. That outcome quality is measurable in your domain at all. Where feedback loops are long, definitions of good are contested, or no counterfactual is available, every argument here degrades to preference. The honest move then is to say so, rather than to instrument something adjacent and call it evidence.

Reference · sixteen specimens

Style is a cognitive-load choice, not a taste one

Every slide does one of three jobs: explain structure, prove a claim, or carry an argument. The job determines the graphics. Below, fifteen established styles and one built for your own constraint, each rendered as a real slide using the same subject matter, so the difference you see is the style and nothing else. Read the strip under each one to see which jobs it can actually carry.

Blueprint / schematic

Borrows the conventions of engineering drawing: graticule ground, dimension lines with end ticks, numbered callouts, a revision block. It signals that the thing has been measured, not sketched.

Best job

Structure with quantities attached; sizing, allocation, capacity.

Audience

Engineering-minded reviewers, and anyone who needs to believe the numbers were checked.

Watch out

The conventions carry authority. Using them on guesses is a form of lying.

Skeuomorphism

Digital things dressed as physical ones: felt, brushed metal, ruled paper, stitched leather, glass with a highlight on it. Bevels and drop shadows imply that elements are objects you could pick up. Dominant in software until roughly 2013, when flat design replaced it.

Best job

Making an unfamiliar thing feel like a familiar object. Warmth and tactility.

Audience

Consumer-facing, nostalgic, or deliberately period material. Rare in enterprise work.

Watch out

Texture competes with content, it dates a deck instantly, and it projects badly. In an architecture deck it reads as a mistake rather than a choice.

15

Neumorphism

Skeuomorphism's 2020 descendant, and a much narrower idea. Everything is one flat colour. Shapes are defined only by a paired light shadow up-left and dark shadow down-right, so elements look extruded from the background or pressed into it. No borders, no fills, no colour contrast.

Capacity controls

Raised means available. Pressed means active. That is the entire vocabulary.

Best job

Almost nothing on a slide. It is a UI aesthetic, and a contested one.

Audience

Design-trend audiences, portfolio pieces, app concept work.

Watch out

It fails WCAG by construction: state is carried by shadow alone, so low vision, projector glare and greyscale printing all erase it. Look at the toggle above and try to tell on from off at a glance.

16

Datum

A system built for the specific constraint the thirteen above do not solve: a deck that is presented live and then forwarded and read alone, carrying structure, proof and argument in one sitting. A datum is the fixed reference plane a survey measures everything from. Here, the plane never moves; only density does. Three registers, one grid, one type scale, one palette. Below, the same system in all three.

Register one, Claim. Above the datum only. The load-bearing word is the single serif italic in the entire deck, so the argument is visible before it is read.

Fixed rules

Datum line never moves. Rail always present. Serif italic only on a claim verb. Amber at most once per slide. All figures tabular mono.

Why it holds together

Register is signalled by a mono word in the corner, not by changing the aesthetic. Density varies; the system does not.

Where it fails

No section break, no keynote moment, no room for a poster slide. Borrow specimen 12 for that, once, and return.
